There's something about Amy Jacobs that makes people feel instantly grounded around her — maybe it's the intention behind every cue she gives, maybe it's the calm confidence she carries into every room she trains in.

Amy has built her reputation on redefining what strength actually looks like — moving away from the aesthetics-first messaging that's dominated fitness culture for years, and toward a definition rooted in capability, consistency, and how a client feels in their body day to day. It's a subtler kind of coaching, one that prioritizes how someone moves and shows up over how a single session looks on camera.

That approach has earned her a genuinely loyal following in a city that tends to reward flash over substance. Clients don't just come back for the workout — they come back for the way Amy makes the work feel purposeful.

We caught up with her to talk training philosophy, and what "strength" means to her beyond the obvious.
