The wellness world has fragmented into a hundred loud, generic voices. What today's fit girl actually wants is quieter and rarer: **taste**.

Not more content. Better filters. Not another supplement. Someone she trusts to say *this one, not that one*. Not a fitness bro screaming about macros. An editor who understands that Pilates and pilaf both count as care.

Fit Girl Things exists in that gap.

## What changed

Ten years ago the wellness girl was a gym-first archetype. Today she is a **cross-category consumer**. She trains four times a week, gets facials, tracks her cycle, drinks matcha, buys vintage, and cares about the room she's walking into on Friday night.

She wants a media brand that speaks all those languages at once. Most don't.

## The three pillars

- **Editorial with a point of view.** Not aggregated, not AI-summarized. Real opinions from people with taste.
- **Real-world energy.** The best content leads to a room. Events aren't a side hustle; they're proof.
- **Trust before scale.** Recommendations mean something because they're personal, not sponsored-first.

## What comes next

Weekly drops. City-based events. A community that forwards the newsletter without being asked.
