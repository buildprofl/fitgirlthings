Red light therapy has fully crossed over from dermatologist's office treatment to at-home wellness staple, with devices now ranging from budget handheld wands to serious full-panel setups that cost as much as a studio membership.

The science behind red and near-infrared light — stimulating collagen production, supporting cellular recovery, calming inflammation — is genuinely well-established. What's less consistent is device quality. Wavelength accuracy, light intensity, and treatment coverage vary enormously across the flood of products now competing in this space, and a poorly built device can mean you're getting a warm glow with none of the actual therapeutic benefit.

The practical takeaway: don't assume every red light device on the market delivers what the category promises. It's worth doing the research on wavelength specs and treatment area before committing, rather than buying on branding and packaging alone.
